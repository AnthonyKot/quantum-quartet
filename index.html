Quantum Mechanics, Explained

A readable guide to the basics of quantum mechanics, for readers coming back to physics.

These essays are for someone who met calculus, vectors and a first physics course some years ago and would like to understand quantum mechanics properly now: not as a list of strange facts, but as a theory whose predictions can be followed and checked. No previous quantum mechanics is assumed.

Each essay starts from one concrete question, such as why a hot poker never glows violet or why a beam of atoms splits in two, and ends by answering it. Along the way it restores the mathematics it needs in a few short reminders, works one example with real numbers, and closes with two questions whose answers are worked out, including the easy wrong turns. Longer derivations sit in fold-out sections: open them for the full argument, or skip them; nothing later depends on them.

Start with the first essay and read in order; later essays use what earlier ones set up. The reading order below differs from the file numbering in a few places, deliberately.
